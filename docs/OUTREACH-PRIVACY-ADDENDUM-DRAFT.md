# VIORA privacy notice — outreach disclosure draft

**Status: DRAFT FOR INDEPENDENT LEGAL REVIEW — NOT PUBLISHED OR APPROVED**

This draft is intended to close the transparency gap identified in the current Privacy Policy. It is not a legal opinion, does not establish a lawful basis for any individual contact, and must not be used to enable outbound sending until counsel has approved the final wording and the corresponding server-side controls are live.

## Business prospect research and direct marketing

VIORA may research organisations that could benefit from its business software, digital services and bespoke digital ecosystems. Where permitted by applicable law, this may include business contact details relating to people acting in a professional capacity, such as a business name, professional name, role, corporate email address, business telephone number, country, organisation website and publicly available information about the organisation's products or operations.

Sources may include the organisation's own website, official business registers, professional directories and other reputable public business sources. VIORA records the source and date of collection, the country, the purpose for which the information is used, the applicable eligibility assessment and any marketing preferences or objections. Public availability alone does not mean that an address is eligible for marketing.

VIORA uses this information to assess whether its services may be relevant to an organisation, prepare individual business-to-business communications, manage enquiries and replies, prevent duplicate contact, honour objections and maintain compliance records. VIORA does not sell prospect contact lists.

## Regional contact rules

VIORA applies country-specific controls before any marketing message is released:

- **Kenya:** VIORA's default approach is consent-first. A business email being publicly listed is not, by itself, treated as consent. Contacts without recorded, valid permission are excluded from outbound marketing.
- **United Kingdom:** Corporate B2B outreach may be considered only where the recipient is confirmed to be a corporate subscriber and the applicable UK GDPR and PECR requirements have been assessed. Sole traders and certain partnerships must not be treated as corporate subscribers. VIORA must identify itself, provide a valid opt-out route and honour objections. A legitimate-interests assessment, where relied upon, must be documented and must not override an individual's absolute right to object to direct marketing.
- **United States:** B2B email is subject to applicable CAN-SPAM requirements, including accurate sender information, non-deceptive subject lines, required sender postal-address disclosures, a functioning opt-out mechanism and timely suppression of opt-outs.
- **Other countries:** Outreach remains disabled until the relevant country's rules, lawful basis, disclosures and opt-out requirements have been reviewed and approved.

These descriptions are operating safeguards, not a substitute for checking the exact recipient type, message, channel and applicable law in each case.

## Lawful basis and individual rights

VIORA records the applicable lawful basis and supporting evidence for each contact and jurisdiction. Depending on the country and circumstances, processing may rely on consent, an existing business relationship, steps requested by the person, or another lawful basis confirmed by a documented assessment. VIORA does not assume that legitimate interests or a public business contact exception is available everywhere.

A person may object to direct marketing or withdraw consent at any time. VIORA will stop marketing to that person and retain only the minimum suppression information needed to ensure that the person is not contacted again. A marketing objection will not be overridden by a later prospect import or a change of campaign.

Privacy requests may be sent to **support@viora.co.ke**. Requests will be handled under the applicable local requirements and, where VIORA acts as a processor for a customer, may be referred to that customer as controller.

## Retention, processors and international transfers

Prospect information should be reviewed for continued relevance and deleted when no longer needed for the documented purpose. The proposed operational retention period is up to 24 months after the last meaningful interaction, unless a shorter period is required or a longer period is justified and documented by law. Minimal suppression records may be retained as necessary to honour an objection.

Outreach records may be processed by VIORA's contracted infrastructure and communications providers, including Cloudflare, Google/Firebase and the email provider used for the campaign. International transfers must use safeguards required by the applicable jurisdiction. Access must be restricted to authorised personnel and recorded for accountability.

## Required legal sign-off before publication

The reviewer must confirm the final wording, the jurisdictions covered, the lawful-basis framework, prospect-source categories, retention periods, international transfer language, rights handling and the actual sender disclosures. The reviewer must also confirm that the production system enforces the same rules, rather than relying on this notice alone.

**Do not publish this draft, mark a jurisdiction approved, or enable campaign sending on the strength of this document.**
